If you want to share it on dedicated scientific platforms to get feedback from physicists and researchers, here are the best options (ranging from formal to informal):

1. ResearchGate (The Academic Social Network)
What it is: The "Facebook for scientists." Researchers upload their preprints, published papers, and raw data here.
Why use it: It’s completely free, requires no endorsement (unlike arXiv), and you can upload your PDF directly. You can add keywords like "Quantum Gravity," "Cosmology," and "Information Theory" so researchers in those fields stumble upon it. It also has a Q&A feature where people can ask you questions about your framework.
How to share: Just create a free account (if you don't have one), go to "Add New," select "Preprint," and upload your Resonance_Cartographer_Paper.pdf.
2. Zenodo (For a Formal DOI)
What it is: An open-access repository hosted by CERN.
Why use it: If you want your paper to be officially "citable" in the academic record, you can upload it here. Zenodo will assign your paper a DOI (Digital Object Identifier). This means if another physicist reads it and wants to reference it in their own paper, they can formally cite your DOI.
How to share: Create a free account, upload the PDF, fill in the metadata (Title, Author: Dominique Reyntjens, etc.), and hit publish.
3. Reddit (For Raw Community Feedback)
What it is: The internet's largest forum.
Why use it: If you want immediate, unfiltered feedback from physics enthusiasts, grad students, and working physicists, Reddit is the place. However, physicists on Reddit can be notoriously strict about speculative theories.
Where to post:
r/cosmology or r/Quantum_Cosmology: These communities are more receptive to theoretical frameworks.
r/hypotheticalphysics: This is specifically designed for speculative theories that aren't yet ready for formal peer review.
(Note: Avoid r/Physics, as they strictly remove self-promoted theoretical papers).
